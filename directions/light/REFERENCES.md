# Light: references

The notes below come from looking at the actual works. Each artist in the brief (20 contemporary, 5 historical) has 2 to 4 named works, a URL for each, and the local image we examined. Images are in `research/<artist>/`, which is gitignored and never published. The hex values were sampled from photographs, so they carry the camera's colour and are not the paint's true colour.

## The concept (round 3)

A Light and Space work is known by what light does in it. The site is one continuous field of light behind every page, at the scale of the room. Every scene sets that field against one precise luminous boundary: a razor horizon, a lens rim, a glass edge, or a cut. Scrolling moves the horizon, so the light rises or falls as a band between scenes and never cross-fades through grey. The palette is taken from the works: the deep dark; one accent family running from black through ember to pale gold (Brindle); and the silvery greys of anodised metal (Ando).

## James Turrell

### Breathing Light (2013), LED light into space, dimensions variable (Ganzfeld)
URL: https://collections.lacma.org/node/1228873 (exhibition: https://www.lacma.org/art/exhibition/james-turrell-breathing-light)
Local image: research/james-turrell/breathing-light-lacma-1.jpg, -2.jpg (magenta/red phase), -3.jpg (blue phase)

- **Where the light comes from:** you can't see a source. The whole aperture looks self-luminous, like a backlit screen. The wall around it (`#3c2e3e`, `#352937`) is lit only by spill from the field, so the frame picks up the field's hue at very low value. It never goes neutral grey.
- **Gradient behaviour (magenta phase):** a centre-weighted radial gradient that is squashed horizontally, with a clear horizon band. Reading down the centre: `#ae01c1` (top, violet) → `#d501b4` → `#ec008b` → `#fe0161` (hot red-pink core) → back through `#ee01aa` → `#b501a6` → `#9d008f` near the floor edge. The hue turns through about 60° (violet→magenta→red) while the value hardly changes, so it's a hue gradient, not a light-to-dark one. The transition is slow and eased with no banding, except for one soft horizontal band about 72% of the way down. That band reads as a horizon or the far floor of the room. In the bottom corners the colour moves toward blue-violet (`#9600d6`), so the corners are cooler than the centre.
- **Inner shape:** in the middle floats a rounded-rectangle "ghost" (corner radius about 10% of its height) that is slightly redder than its surroundings. Its edge contrast is tiny, maybe 3–5% in hue and almost none in value, so you notice it and then lose it. Two faint vertical seams in the ghost come from the LED fixtures and look like refraction.
- **Blue phase:** almost monochrome. `#2262fa` at the top, `#0146e5` at the centre, `#2b67fc` in the lower corners. The centre is slightly darker and more saturated than the edges here, the opposite of the magenta phase. There is also a pale "floor glow" band along the bottom (`#2b67fc` above `#0144d8`).
- **Edges:** the aperture edge is razor-sharp against the wall. There's no bevel and no visible thickness. Because the wall is so dark, the field looks like a flat plane of colour cut into it. It doesn't read as a window onto depth.
- **Surface:** none visible. There is no texture at all, only light.
- **Scale/body:** room-sized. The dark stair in the foreground shows the aperture is wider than a person can reach. You walk *into* it.

### Afrum I (White) (1967), projected light (cross-corner projection)
URL: https://www.guggenheim.org/artwork/4084 (the related Afrum (White), 1966, is at LACMA: https://collections.lacma.org/object/140816. That page returned no image to curl, so the photo I saw is the Guggenheim's Afrum I)
Local image: research/james-turrell/afrum-i-white-guggenheim.jpg

- **Where the light comes from:** a projector you can't see throws a hexagon across a room corner, and it reads as a glowing *solid cube* floating in front of the corner.
- **Gradient:** the face is almost flat white (`#e8eaed` left face, `#ececf1` right face). The two "faces" differ by only about 2% in value, and that tiny difference is enough to make them read as planes of a cube. Just outside the hard edge there is a narrow halo (`#c8cacc` → `#a9acab`) falling off to the wall (`#8a8a84`). The halo is roughly 5–8% of the cube's width, and past it the wall darkens smoothly toward the room corners (`#656559`). It's a classic bloom: sharp shape, soft glow.
- **Edges:** a crisp, perfect hexagon. The line where the two walls meet runs through the middle of the cube and is nearly invisible inside the light, then reappears above and below it.
- **Colour:** light warm-cool grey room, cool white light. There's no hue in this piece. The illusion comes entirely from value.
- **Scale:** the cube is roughly head-sized from the viewing distance. The room is otherwise empty.

### Twilight Epiphany (2012), Skyspace, Rice University, Houston
URL: https://moody.rice.edu/james-turrell-twilight-epiphany-skyspace (photos: https://www.designboom.com/art/james-turrell-twilight-epiphany-skyspace-at-rice-university/)
Local image: research/james-turrell/twilight-epiphany-rice-2.jpg, -6.jpg, -11.jpg

- **Where the light comes from:** LEDs hidden along the roof edge light the underside of a thin, knife-edged canopy. The square opening in the canopy shows plain sky.
- **The key effect (image -6):** the canopy is lit lilac-pink (`#eacfef`) and the sky in the opening reads as a flat, saturated blue (`#0067cb`). The sky looks like a *painted flat plane set into the ceiling*, not something far away. The opening edge is a hard knife edge with no visible thickness, so nothing tells you how deep the sky is.
- **Gradient on the canopy:** there are two brightness peaks, one halfway between the outer rim and the opening, and one just inside the opening, then it dims toward the outer rim (`#bb93d5`) and toward the opening's own edge (`#b591d5`). It's a soft eased falloff with no banding. In the orange phase (-11) the canopy goes from amber `#bd5315` at the edges to `#b54b03`, with the sky a pale grey-blue `#c5cbd0`. In the pink phase (-2), `#e65d83`/`#de5880` against a grey sky `#888c98`. The *complement* trick is visible: warm canopy makes the sky look cooler and bluer than it is, and violet canopy makes the sky look electric blue.
- **Scale/body:** the photos show people seated in rows or on stairs, looking up. The canopy floats on thin white columns. You view it from inside a grass berm, and the work is timed to sunrise and sunset.

### Raemar Magenta (1968–69 series; this photo 2016), light installation (Shallow Space Construction)
URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Raemar_Magenta.JPG (Houghton Hall, 2016, CC BY-SA 4.0)
Local image: research/james-turrell/raemar-magenta.jpg

- **Light source:** a hidden strip of light runs around a false wall set in front of the real back wall. It washes the gap between them, so the inner rectangle becomes a hard-edged "hole" of colour, and the lit rim looks like a glowing frame.
- **Colours:** the inner field is deep violet (`#372089`) and uniform. The glowing rim clips to near-white lavender (`#e6defa`). The surrounding reveal is a mid violet (`#5536d5` → `#4226af` further out). So the value steps are: dark core → blown-out white ring → mid violet surround. The ring blooms on both sides, softly outward and a little inward.
- **Edges:** the inner edge of the white band is very sharp. Its outer edge feathers into the reveal over about 3–4% of the width.
- **Takeaway:** this is Turrell's "cut-out" logic, where one rectangle of light makes the wall look like it has a void in it.

**Takeaways for the site:**
- **Frameless aperture:** a rounded or rectangular panel with **no border and no shadow**, set on a background that is the field's own hue at about 10–15% lightness (e.g. field `#e0009f` on a page of `#2a1f2d`). The panel should look emitted, not placed.
- **Hue-shift gradient, not a value gradient:** build the field as a squashed radial gradient (e.g. `radial-gradient(120% 70% at 50% 45%, #fe0161, #ec008b 30%, #c400b0 60%, #a800bf 100%)`). Lightness stays nearly constant while hue turns about 60°, with no banding. Add a single very soft horizontal band (a 2–4% lighter strip, blurred about 8% of the height) at roughly 70% height as a "horizon".
- **Ghost shape:** a rounded rectangle inside the field with contrast under 5% and an edge blur of about 1–2% of its size. Readers find it, then lose it.
- **Afrum bloom:** a sharp-edged shape plus a thin outer glow (`box-shadow: 0 0 0.6em ...` at low alpha) inside a vignetted dark surround. A 2% value difference between two facets is enough to suggest 3-D.
- **Skyspace complement:** a flat saturated rectangle (the "sky") sitting in a larger lit field of its complementary tint, lilac `#eacfef` around blue `#0067cb`. Give the opening a hard edge with 0 px of bevel. Slowly animating the surround's hue over minutes (sunset timing) changes how the unchanged centre looks.

## Robert Irwin

### Untitled (1968), acrylic lacquer on Plexiglas (disc), 53 1/4 × 53 1/4 × 24 1/2 in.
URL: https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/70.5/
Local image: research/robert-irwin/disc-sfmoma-1968.jpg
(Also seen: Untitled (1969), acrylic lacquer on cast acrylic, Anderson Collection at Stanford. https://anderson.stanford.edu/collection/untitled-by-robert-irwin/, research/robert-irwin/disc-anderson.jpg. That image is small, about 300 px, but shows the same stripe logic.)

- **Where the light comes from:** four floor or ceiling spots hit the disc from four diagonal directions. The disc looks lit *from within*: its centre is the brightest thing in the frame (`#f9fefc`), whiter and slightly cooler than the wall (`#bfbcb7` to `#d5d8d6`).
- **The shadow set:** four large overlapping shadow copies of the disc, each about 1.8× the disc's diameter, offset to the four diagonals (upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right). Where they overlap they form a quatrefoil or cloverleaf. Each shadow is very pale (the wall inside a shadow is about `#c7c6c3`, against unshadowed wall at about `#d5d8d6`, a value drop of roughly 5%). Shadow edges are soft but *legible* (penumbra about 1–2% of the disc width). Where two shadows overlap it gets slightly darker again (`#c1bdb7`). So there are three value levels: wall, single shadow, double shadow.
- **Edge of the disc:** the rim almost disappears. The disc's edge (`#d7d7d2` at the left rim) is the same value as the lit wall next to it, so you only find the circle from the faint change in texture and the shadows around it. Irwin also sprays the disc darker toward the rim, so it fades into the wall.
- **The stripe:** a horizontal band about 5% of the disc's height, running across the full diameter. It's darkest at the centre (`#796c5c`, a warm grey-bronze) and fades symmetrically to near wall colour at the ends (`#cac8c3`, `#d1d0c9`). The fade is steep near the middle, flat toward the ends, with no hard edge. Where the disc has no paint, the stripe is clear acrylic, and the stripe's shadows reappear in the four shadow copies as faint pale bars.
- **Surface:** matte, sprayed, a soft sheen with no specular hot spot.
- **Scale:** about 4.5 ft across, floating about 2 ft off the wall on a hidden arm, hung at eye level.

### Scrim veil—Black rectangle—Natural light (1977; reinstalled Whitney 2013), scrim, black tape/paint, natural light
URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Robert_Irwin_Scrim_Veil_Black_Rectangle_Natural_Light_Whitney_2013.jpg (Whitney: https://whitney.org/artists/642)
Local image: research/robert-irwin/scrim-veil-whitney-2013.jpg

- **Light source:** the Breuer building's single trapezoid window, entering at the far wall. That window is the brightest thing in the room (`#d0d0d0`, blown out), and it throws a long, bright reflection across the stone floor.
- **The scrim:** a translucent white fabric plane hangs from the ceiling and stops at a thin black band at about eye level, roughly 1.6 m. From below and at an angle it reads as a solid ceiling or wall (`#767b7f` in shadow). Where the window light hits it, it turns *see-through*: you can see the brick building outside through the scrim (the top-right window area). The same material is opaque or transparent depending only on the light behind it.
- **The black line:** a razor-thin horizontal band (the scrim's lower edge) runs the full length of the room. It is the only drawn line in the work, and it sets a false horizon at head height. Visitors walk under it.
- **Contrast:** very high overall, with a near-black ceiling grid (`#1b1b1a`), mid-grey scrim, white window, and shiny grey floor (`#b3a8a1` where the light reflects). The space feels huge, and the people look small and silhouetted.

### untitled (dawn to dusk) (2016), permanent building with scrim walls, Chinati Foundation, Marfa
URL: https://chinati.org/collection/robert-irwin/
Local image: research/robert-irwin/chinati-dawn-to-dusk-b.jpg (photo: Alex Marks). I also looked at two exterior photos but didn't keep them because they don't show the light.

- **Light source:** a row of tall, narrow windows along one wall. Low sun comes through and lays tall salmon-orange rectangles (`#c88264`, `#d18b71`) on a long white scrim wall that runs down the middle of the corridor.
- **What happens:** the rectangles repeat and shrink with perspective down the corridor. Each one is a soft-edged parallelogram. The edges are *slightly* feathered where the light crosses the scrim's fibre, and sharper where it lands on the solid plaster. Between them the scrim is a dull mauve-grey (`#7a5f51` to `#867066`) with a faint double image, because you see the solid wall behind the scrim through it.
- **Colour meeting:** warm light patches on a cool, dim ground. The patches feel like colour *on* the surface, more than light passing *through* it.
- **Scale:** architectural. The corridor is roughly 30 m long, and the viewer walks the length of it.

### Untitled (Acrylic Column) (1969–2011), cast acrylic, 15' 6 1/2" × 9" × 3 1/2"
URL: https://www.whitecube.com/artworks/untitled-acrylic-column
Local image: research/robert-irwin/acrylic-column-wc-1.jpg (installation view, two columns and a visitor), research/robert-irwin/acrylic-column-wc-2.jpg (base detail)

- **Almost invisible:** in the room shot the columns nearly vanish against a white wall and grey concrete. What gives them away is refraction: a vertical strip where the visitor behind is *displaced and cut off* (his head disappears above a certain height, and his legs are offset sideways). Otherwise there's only a thin glint along one edge.
- **Base detail:** the V (chevron) cross-section is visible from above. The concrete floor, seen through the acrylic, turns into repeated lobed shapes at the base (caustics and internal reflections). There's a bright white edge line at the bottom where light is trapped inside.
- **Takeaway:** the column shows itself only by what it does to whatever is behind it.

**Takeaways for the site:**
- **Irwin disc:** a circle whose fill is 1–2% brighter than the page at its centre and *equal* to the page at its rim (a radial gradient from `#f9fefc` to page colour `#d5d8d6`), so it has no visible edge. Put four large, very pale shadow copies behind it, offset diagonally (e.g. four `radial-gradient` circles about 1.8× the disc size at ±35% / ±35% offsets, each only about 5% darker than the page, with a small blur of about 1% of the disc). You get a quatrefoil where they overlap and the disc itself seems to float.
- **Fading stripe:** a horizontal rule that is darkest at the centre and fades to nothing at both ends (`linear-gradient(90deg, transparent, #796c5c 50%, transparent)`, eased so the dark part is short). It makes a good section divider.
- **Scrim line:** one hairline black rule at a fixed viewport height, running the full width, as a false horizon that everything else sits under. Put panels above it at about 8–15% opacity so content behind shows through only where it is "lit".
- **Refraction column:** a tall, narrow, transparent strip that offsets whatever is behind it by a few pixels (`backdrop-filter` plus a small translate, or an SVG displacement filter in a vertical band), with a 1 px highlight on one edge. Otherwise it is invisible.
- **Window light on scrim:** a repeating row of soft, warm (`#d18b71`) parallelograms shrinking with perspective over a dim mauve ground. It works as a slow scroll-driven light patch.

## Larry Bell

### Cube (1966), vacuum-coated glass with chrome-plated metal frame
URL: https://collections.lacma.org/object/39951
Local image: research/larry-bell/cube-1966-lacma-1.jpg (dark backdrop), research/larry-bell/cube-1966-lacma-2.jpg (light backdrop)

- **Where the light comes from:** no source of its own. The colour is *interference*: the thin metallic film on the glass reflects some wavelengths and passes others, depending on viewing angle and film thickness.
- **Gradient behaviour (dark backdrop):** the front face runs diagonally from warm ochre-tan at the upper left (`#9c876c`) through mauve (`#8f7d83`) and violet (`#777696`) to steel blue (`#5d7a9c`, `#6d8da1`) at the centre seam, then to a pale lilac-white haze (`#9fa9b5`) below. The bottom sits in greenish grey-olive (`#6f7065`, `#898675`). The colours arrive in *soft diagonal bands* like oil on water: each hue fills maybe 15–20% of the face, and they blend smoothly with no hard lines. The side face is much less coloured, mostly neutral warm grey (`#8b8679`). The effect depends on angle, so faces at different angles show different amounts of colour.
- **Light backdrop (-2):** the same kind of cube looks almost clear. Iridescence only survives as a faint rainbow wash (blue-violet-pink) near one vertical edge and along the inside of the frame. Against a light ground the effect nearly disappears. The dark background is what makes the colour visible.
- **Transparency and reflection:** you see the inner faces and the far edges through the front, doubled and tinted. The top pane reflects the room and turns a dark teal-grey.
- **Edges:** the frame is bright polished chrome, the only hard, specular element, and it outlines the soft colour wash.
- **Scale:** tabletop to plinth-height, about 12–16 in. A cube you look *into*.

### Untitled (1964), bismuth, chromium, gold and rhodium on glass, gold-plated brass frame (Hirshhorn)
URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:UntitledGoldBox1964.jpg (CC BY 3.0)
Local image: research/larry-bell/untitled-gold-box-1964.jpg

- An earlier, warmer, more opaque cube: amber-bronze panes you can only partly see through. On a clear plinth it seems to hover. You can make out a pattern of lines inside (earlier patterned coating, or reflections through the panes). Every face reads as a smoky, tinted mirror, darker at the base and warmer toward the top where the room light hits.

### 6 x 6 An Improvisation (2014 at Chinati; 2015–16 White Cube, Miami), coated and uncoated glass panels, 36 panels as standing walls
URL: https://chinati.org/exhibitions/larry-bell-6-x-6-an-improvisation/ and https://www.whitecube.com/gallery-exhibitions/6-x-6-an-improvisation
Local image: research/larry-bell/6x6-chinati-1.jpg, 6x6-chinati-2.jpg (small, 450 px, but clear), 6x6-whitecube-miami.jpg
Related: Standing Walls I (1968/2017), Dia Beacon, https://diaart.org/collection/collection/bell-larry-standing-walls-i-19682017-2017-031 (I couldn't get an image of this one).

- **Structure:** pairs of room-height glass panels meet at 90° corners (L-shapes), set in sequence down a long white gallery.
- **Gradient behaviour:** each coated panel runs from *clear at the top* to *dark smoky grey at the bottom*. It's a vertical gradient, fast at the bottom and eased toward the top. Where panels overlap in your line of sight, the grey adds up, so you get stepped bands of darker grey where two, three or four layers stack. The Chinati photo shows a four-step "tunnel" of nested grey rectangles going back in depth, like a cheap infinity mirror made only from partial reflection.
- **Reflection:** the dark lower halves act as mirrors. In the Miami photo the panels hold the trees outside, the window mullions, and the columns of the room at once, reflected and seen through, so it's impossible to tell which is which. The reflected image is *softened and dimmed* by the tint, not sharp.
- **Edges:** the panels have no frame and nearly invisible edges. You only find the edges where the grey tints overlap.
- **Scale:** each panel is about 8 ft tall, more than body height. You walk between them and see yourself faintly in them.

**Takeaways for the site:**
- **Interference wash:** on a *dark* background, a translucent pane with a diagonal multi-stop gradient (`linear-gradient(135deg, #9c876c 0%, #8f7d83 25%, #777696 45%, #5d7a9c 60%, #9fa9b5 80%, #6f7065 100%)` at about 50–70% opacity). Each colour gets a wide stop and the transitions stay soft. Shift the gradient angle a few degrees with scroll or pointer so the colour "moves" the way it does when you walk around the cube. Keep the effect subtle on light backgrounds, because it disappears there, as it does on the real object.
- **Chrome hairline:** a 1–2 px bright neutral edge (`#e8e8e8` with a darker inner line) around a soft-coloured pane. It gives the only hard edge.
- **Standing-wall stacking:** a few overlapping frameless panels, each `linear-gradient(to bottom, transparent, rgba(20,20,22,.55))`, offset in depth. Where they overlap, the darkness adds up into visible steps. It's a cheap, honest way to show depth or layers with no borders.
- **Tint-mirror:** panels that show a dimmed, blurred copy of the content behind them (a blurred backdrop at reduced brightness) in their lower half only.

## Helen Pashgian

Note: the brief suggested "Lenses (LACMA 2014)". LACMA's 2014 show *Helen Pashgian: Light Invisible* was actually built around a row of large columns, Untitled (2012–13). The lenses appear in other shows (e.g. *Spheres and Lenses*, Lehmann Maupin NY 2021; *New Lenses and Spheres*, Vito Schnabel). **I couldn't get a usable image of a lens work:** the Lehmann Maupin *Spheres and Lenses* page returned 404, Ocula returned 403, and the Brooklyn Rail image was a portrait drawing, which I deleted. From text sources only: the lenses are large wall-mounted cast-epoxy discs, about 45 in. across, frosted, with colour in the centre that fades out along the radius.

### Untitled (2012–13), cast epoxy / formed acrylic columns (Light Invisible, LACMA 2014)
URL: https://www.lacma.org/art/exhibition/helen-pashgian-light-invisible
Local image: research/helen-pashgian/columns-light-invisible-lacma-2014.jpg

- **Where the light comes from:** a spotlight above each column, in an otherwise black room. The light seems to *enter the top and pour down inside*. The top cap glows almost white (`#f7f7f7`), and the column dims through `#dee0e1` to mid grey (`#909291`) and about `#6d6d6d` near the floor. The top 20% is the brightest and the fade below it is long and eased, as if light were diffusing through milk.
- **Internal forms:** each column holds a small shape embedded partway down: a dimple, a pink-orange wedge, a green lens-shaped glow (`#eff5ed` highlight on a darker body), a bluish orb. They are visible only as soft, out-of-focus shapes of colour inside the frosted surface. Their edges are completely feathered, as if seen through fog. Some catch light and glow, others are just faintly tinted.
- **Surface:** frosted and matte, like sandblasted glass or wax, with no reflections, so the light looks trapped inside the material.
- **Shadows and floor:** each column casts a crisp circular pool of light on the floor, with the column's own shadow as a crescent inside it. The row recedes in strict perspective into black.
- **Scale:** about 8 ft (2.5 m) tall, taller than a person, spaced so you walk past them one at a time.

### Untitled (green), Untitled (orange), Untitled (grey) (2009), cast epoxy columns
URL: https://www.lehmannmaupin.com/exhibitions/in-focus-helen-pashgian
Local image: research/helen-pashgian/columns-2009-lehmann-maupin.jpg

- Each column is a pair of overlapping cylinders forming a figure-8 cross-section. In the **orange** one, the top edge glows almost red-orange (`#e35a24`) and deepens to `#c33615` and `#a03215` where the two lobes meet, so the colour *concentrates* in the fold and the thicker section. The **green** one runs from almost black-green at the top in shadow (`#1d2a1e`) to a paler olive-yellow near the base (`#525733`) where the floor light enters. The **grey** one has a small warm orange glow embedded at the top, like a coal inside ash.
- Colour depth follows material thickness: colour is densest where there is more resin. The long floor shadows are warm, and the columns glow against pale walls.

### Spheres (dates not given on the page I read), cast epoxy / resin
URL: https://www.lehmannmaupin.com/artists/helen-pashgian (images labelled "Sphere by Helen Pashgian")
Local image: research/helen-pashgian/sphere-lm-a.jpg, research/helen-pashgian/sphere-lm-b.jpg

- **sphere-lm-a (frosted, about 1600 px):** a jade-teal ball on white. **Takeaways for the site:**

- **Frosted edge:** give circles or pills a 1–2% blurred edge (e.g. `mask-image: radial-gradient(circle, #000 97%, transparent 100%)`), so objects look just out of focus: solid, but not quite graspable.
- **Coloured shadow:** a shadow that carries the object's hue at low saturation (`box-shadow: 0 30px 40px -20px rgba(0,181,167,.35)`) instead of grey, as if light has gone through the object.
- **Colour depth follows thickness:** where two shapes overlap or a form folds, deepen the colour (orange `#e35a24` → `#a03215` in the overlap). `mix-blend-mode: multiply` on overlapping tinted shapes does this for free.

### Green Wedge (1969), cast polyester resin, 14 1/2 × 9 1/2 × 8 3/4 in.
URL: https://peteralexander.com/portfolio-view/greenwedge1969castresin14-1_2x9-1_2x8-3_4_9dd0/ (image from https://peteralexanderestate.com/early-resin)

- **Where the light comes from:** the wall and room light behind and above it, passing *through* the resin. The thin top edge lets almost all the light through and looks nearly clear. Lower down the form is thicker and absorbs more.

### Cloud Box (1966), cast resin with embedded clouds; Pink-Green Slab (1967), cast polyester resin
URL: https://peteralexanderestate.com/early-resin
Local image: research/peter-alexander/cloud-box-1966.jpg, research/peter-alexander/pink-green-slab-1967.jpg

- **Cloud Box:** an amber resin cube (`#8d6f49` to `#6b5033`) holding milky cloud-like forms suspended *inside*. You see the top face as a flat, faintly reflective plane (`#d9c9b3`). The side faces show the clouds, soft-edged and billowy, floating at several depths. Internal refraction means the clouds on one face are echoed and shifted on the next.
- **Pink-Green Slab:** a low, flat block where colours stack in *depth* rather than across the surface. You see pink-mauve (`#c79f98`) through the thick body, olive-tan (`#c2b39f`) at the thin edges and corners, and dusky rose (`#a28082`) on the front face. **Takeaways for the site:**
- **Absorption gradient:** for something that "fills with colour", use a steep exponential falloff rather than a linear one. In CSS, many stops approximating `exp()`: e.g. `linear-gradient(to bottom, #e0e2d5 0%, #9fb3a0 10%, #698770 20%, #334637 30%, #131f16 40%, #060606 55%)`. The top edge must match the background exactly so it dissolves.
- **One soft edge, three hard edges:** a slab whose top edge fades completely into the page while its other edges stay crisp.
- **Edge-tinted colour:** a translucent block whose *edges* are a different hue from its body (olive rim, pink core). Do this with an inner `box-shadow` in a second hue, or a border gradient, at low opacity.
- **Nested ghost frames:** a translucent panel showing a faint inner rectangle inset about 6–10%, like the back face seen through resin. It gives depth with no drop shadow.

## Cross-artist summary (what's reproducible)
1. **No frame, matched surround** (Turrell Breathing Light, Raemar): the field's hue carried into a very dark surround, with a razor edge and no border.
2. **Edge that vanishes into the ground** (Irwin disc rim, Alexander wedge top, Pashgian frosted sphere): the edge value equals the background value, so the form is found only by its interior gradient and shadows.
3. **Four soft shadow copies** (Irwin): a quatrefoil of pale diagonal shadows, three value levels.
4. **Hue shift at constant value** (Turrell): gradients that turn hue about 60° without changing lightness.
5. **Absorption curves** (Alexander, Pashgian columns): exponential, not linear, and thickness means more colour.
6. **Interference sheen on dark** (Bell): wide, soft diagonal colour bands on a translucent pane, visible only over dark backgrounds, shifting with angle.
7. **Transparency that shows itself only by distortion** (Irwin column, Bell standing walls): refraction offsets and stacked grey tints, no outline.

### Hakanai Fleeting Sea Sky Blue (2013), hand-dyed anodized aluminum, 48 x 48 in
URL: https://www.sundaramtagore.com/exhibitions/miya-ando-mujo-impermanence/selected-works?view=slider
Local image: research/miya-ando/hakanai-fleeting-sea-sky-blue-2013.jpg

- **Composition:** a square made of two horizontal plates stacked on top of each other. The physical seam sits at about 49% of the height and is the horizon. The work is effectively a sky above and its reflection below, with the two halves nearly mirrored.
- **Where the light is:** the brightest zone is not at the top. It is a pale, slightly warm grey-cyan haze close to the seam: #A0A7A8 just above it, #ADB5B2 to #B1BBB8 just below. The values darken toward both the top (#457C8A) and the bottom (#344F58). So the light sits in the middle, as it does over water at dusk, and seems to come from inside the plate rather than falling on it.
- **Gradient:** vertical top to bottom: #457C8A → #6EAEBA → #87B7BF → #9CB0B3 → #A0A7A8 (seam) → #A1B5B6 → #86B0B7 → #5D8A93 → #335863 → #344F58. It is eased, and not symmetric. Above the seam the change is gentle and steady. Below it, the value holds pale until about 80% of the height and then falls hard over roughly 8% into a dark teal band. There is also a sideways drift: the left third is lighter (#91B2B7) and the right edge darker (#61888E), which reads as off-axis light.
- **Hue:** the blue turns to a desaturated warm grey at the horizon, with a hint of mauve just above the seam. The haze is less saturated than the sky, which is what gives it an atmospheric look.
- **Edges:** the seam is hard. The upper plate darkens slightly in the last 2–3% before its edge (#A5A9A8) and then steps up to the lighter lower plate. The outer edges are clean, machined metal with no frame.
- **Surface:** anodized metal. In the dark bottom band, fine **vertical striations** are visible where the dye ran and pooled, like a brushed-metal grain crossing the horizontal gradient. Elsewhere the colour is smooth, with no brushwork.
- **Reflection:** the dye sits inside the anodized layer, so the metal sheen shows through the colour. The photograph shows no reflection of the room.
- **Idea for the web:** a horizon made of two stacked blocks that meet at a 1px hard seam. The upper block darkens by about 3% luminance in the 12px above the seam. The lower block starts about 4% lighter. Both have eased gradients that run *away* from the seam, so the light gathers at the line. For the dark band at the bottom, add a vertical-only noise (for example `feTurbulence` with baseFrequency of about "0.9 0.01") at 4–6% opacity, which makes it read as brushed metal.

Local image: research/miya-ando/sui-getsu-ka-gold-2013.jpg

- **Composition:** three vertical panels separated by gaps about 1.5% of the width. The wall shows through the gaps as white stripes.
- **Light:** each outer panel has a soft **radial hotspot** at about 40% of the height: #C8AF74 at the centre against #B09445 at the top and #927B49 at the bottom. Use a radial-gradient ellipse at 50% 40% going from #C8AF74 to #A88F4E, with a second, very large ellipse that darkens the lower quarter toward #8C7D5D. So the two panels look lit by one source behind the gap between them.

- **Idea for the web:** light that seems to come from behind a split. Two adjacent panels, each with a radial-gradient hotspot centred on its *inner* top edge (circle at 100% 22% for the left panel, 0% 22% for the right), using #C0583A → #8E1D1F. ### Meditation Blue Black (2013), hand-dyed anodized aluminum, 48 x 48 in (diptych)
URL: same Mujo page
Local image: research/miya-ando/meditation-blue-black-2013.jpg

- **This is the clearest horizon glow of the set.** The field is blue-black, #1C2935 at the top and #0D161D at the bottom, which is darker below than above. A single **electric-blue band (#004777, rising to about #0A5A9A at its brightest)** runs across both panels at about 52% of the height. Its core is about 4% of the height, and it feathers out over roughly another 4% above and below.
- The band's ends taper and fade before the outer edges of the work, like a lens-shaped ellipse. It crosses the central gap with no break, so the gap reads as a slice through one continuous light.

It should go wider when you scroll, never taller.

**Takeaways for the site:**
- Light lives at the horizon or seam, not at the top. The brightest and least saturated value sits where two fields meet.
- A hard physical seam (a 1px edge, or a real gap that shows the background) sitting on a soft gradient is Ando's signature contrast. The gradients are feathered almost to nothing, while the edges of the object are machined and exact.
- Eased, asymmetric falloff: long gentle fades with one abrupt drop near an edge (Hakanai's bottom band).
- A faint vertical grain across a horizontal gradient reads as material, like dyed metal, rather than digital.
- Palette anchors: #457C8A, #9CB0B3, #A0A7A8, #335863 (sea-sky); #004777 on #1C2935 (blue-black); #8E1D1F, #AD442D, #9F8572 (red).

### Ukrainian Quartet – Snake Island (2023), acrylic on canvas, 84 x 84 in (213.4 x 213.4 cm)

## Cross-artist patterns worth reusing

1. **The brightest thing is a seam or ring, not a fill:** Ando's horizon haze, Niesche's pale ring between complementaries, Bussières' bloom around a ring. Draw light as a thin, pale, desaturated band where two fields meet.
2. **Hard object edge, vanishing colour edge:** Ando's plate seams, Niesche's 1px brass rim, Montiel's panel seam. Keep container borders crisp and gradients soft.
3. **One light across a split:** Ando's Meditation Red, Montiel's Light Within Darkness, the Bussières grid. A single glow crossing a gap or gutter ties separate panels into one field.
4. **Texture that is directional or chromatic, never grey:** vertical dye streaks (Ando), fabric dot weave (Niesche), horizontal scan strokes (Koop), multicolour spray grain (Montiel).

### Geodesy 1514 (2023)
URL: http://www.andymoses.com/ (listed only as "Geodesy 1514, 2023"; I could not confirm medium or dimensions. Other works in the series are acrylic on canvas over a circular wood panel.)

- A monochrome version of the radial burst: metallic gold (`#e1a910` in the highlights, `#b4760f` in the mids, `#854805` and `#4d3613` in the shadows), with thin deep-navy veins (about `#101c3a`) as the only contrast.
- This is the clearest example of the **metallic sheen**. The gold changes value by 60–90 L points across a single ribbon, depending on which way the ridges face. It reads as polished brass or satin, not as flat yellow.

Takeaways for the site:
- **Satin-ribbon sheen:** give a band of colour a *cross-section* gradient that runs perpendicular to its length: dark edge → lighter centre → dark edge, for example `#142890 → #3c4eb5 (at 45%) → #142890`. - **Hairline-banded horizon:** make a pale "fog" band from a `repeating-linear-gradient` of 1–2px stripes that alternate between two or three near-identical greys (`#bfc4c6`, `#c5c0b6`, `#b6bab8`), about 30–40% of the height, with busy saturated colour above and below. The stripes add brushed-metal life to what would otherwise be a flat band.
- **Iridescence on a budget:** animate a thin band's hue from teal to violet along its length (a conic or linear gradient whose stops drift slowly with scroll). This mimics interference pigment shifting with viewing angle.

## Ruth Pastine

Pastine paints in oil on canvas stretched over *bevelled* stretchers and panels, so the edge pulls in and the painting seems to float. Each work is made of vertical soft-edged bands, usually pairs of complementary colours.

### Blue Light 1, Blue Light Series (2023), oil on canvas on beveled panel, 90 x 48 x 2.5 in
URL: https://ruthpastine.com/light-as-air/
Local image: research/ruth-pastine/blue-light-1-2023.jpg

- **Near-monochrome ultramarine.** Measured across the middle, left edge to centre: edge `#20327f` (L 51) → a **luminous ridge** of `#345bde` (L 92) about 14% in from the edge → a slightly *dimmer* centre plateau `#2643cc` (L 70). The right side mirrors this.
- So the brightest point is **not** the centre and not the edge. It is a soft vertical ridge 12–15% in from each side. The canvas reads as a gently curved **convex cylinder**, as if lit from the front with a highlight on each shoulder.
- There is also a slow top-to-bottom shift: slightly brighter and more violet-blue at the top, deeper toward the bottom.
- **Edges:** the canvas edge is crisp against the white wall. The bevel hides the stretcher, so the painting's dark edge meets the wall with no visible side, and the field seems to hover.
- **Surface:** matte oil, with the fine canvas weave visible close up. No gloss at all. The luminosity comes entirely from the value structure.

### Blue (Orange), Light as Air Series (2022), oil on canvas on beveled stretcher, 60 x 32 x 2.5 in
URL: https://ruthpastine.com/light-as-air/
Local image: research/ruth-pastine/blue-orange-light-as-air-2022.jpg

- **Complementary crossing that makes light.** Measured across the middle, from the left edge:
  - Cadmium orange `#e1711c` (L 130).
  - Warm sand `#d49764`.

- The crossing zone takes about 20% of the painting's width on each side (orange fully gone to blue fully in). The neutral peak sits almost exactly halfway through the crossing. Where the two complements cancel, the eye reads *white light*, not grey mud.

### Red (Green), Light as Air Series (2022), oil on canvas on beveled stretcher, 60 x 32 x 2.5 in
URL: https://ruthpastine.com/light-as-air/
Local image: research/ruth-pastine/red-green-light-as-air-2022.jpg

- The same structure: an outer yellow-green `#73b32a` (L 155) → a lime flash `#ccde75` → **cream seam `#e1dba7`/`#e6d3c0` (L ≈ 215, the brightest point in the painting)** → blush `#e79fb1` → a flat rose-pink centre `#e389a2` (L 157).
- Here the light seam is warmer and brighter than in the blue/orange piece. The green side also goes *lighter and yellower* just before the seam (`#a7d145` → `#ccde75`), so the crossing isn't symmetrical. The green "rises" into light, then settles into pink.

### Blue (Orange), Presence Absence Series (2021), oil on canvas on beveled stretcher, 48 x 48 x 2.5 in
URL: https://ruthpastine.com/light-as-air/
Local image: research/ruth-pastine/blue-orange-presence-absence-2021.jpg

- A square version with softer colour. Salmon edges `#fcc9b2` (L 210) → pearl `#e5cabd` → cool grey `#c4d1d7` → `#82bee0` → cerulean `#25a7df` (L 143), which then stays perfectly flat across the middle 50%.
- The crossing again takes about 18–20% of the width per side. Here the edges are *lighter* than the centre, so the frame of pale warm light makes the blue look like a deep, recessed window.

Takeaways for the site:
- **Complementary crossing with a light seam:** from an edge colour to its complement, add a neutral stop at the crossing that is *lighter than both*:
  `linear-gradient(90deg, #e1711c 0%, #d49764 10%, #bcc0c2 18%, #92bad4 24%, #6fbae5 32%, #6fbae5 68%, #92bad4 76%, #bcc0c2 82%, #d49764 90%, #e1711c 100%)`.
  Place the neutral stop at the midpoint of a transition roughly 20% of the width wide. For the warm version, use a cream stop (`#e6d3c0`) between `#73b32a` and `#e389a2`.
- **Shoulder highlights (convex illusion):** for a monochrome field, put the brightest stop 12–15% in from each side, not at the centre:
  `#20327f 0%, #345bde 14%, #2643cc 30%, #2643cc 70%, #345bde 86%, #20327f 100%`.
  The panel then reads as a softly lit cylinder.
- **No texture, no gloss:** Pastine's glow comes from value placement alone. Keep these fields matte, with no noise and a crisp rectangular edge on a flat background. At most add a 1px darker edge and a soft offset shadow to suggest the bevel's float.
- **Slow vertical drift:** add a second, very weak top→bottom darkening (about 10–15 L points) so the bands feel lit from above.

## Dion Johnson

Dion Johnson paints in acrylic on canvas: large sprayed gradient fields cut by hard-edged arcs, tapering "drip" forms and thin vertical stripes. The compositions read like light through blinds, or a digital glitch of a sunset.

URL: https://www.lalouver.com/rogue-wave-exhibition.cfm?tExhibition_id=2167

- **Structure:** big sweeping arcs (segments of large circles, like the edges of sails or parabolas) divide the canvas into zones. Dozens of thin vertical elements overlay them:

  - wider bars that taper to rounded bottoms like drips, in violet `#5a24b0` and cobalt.
  - Some bars are translucent, and the arcs show through them.
- **Gradients are vertical, and each shape has its own.**

## Scott Sueme

Canadian painter based in Vancouver. His hard-edged, hand-cut shapes sit on flat grounds. His recent "Field in the Wind" show (Uprise Art, New York, 2025) moved from grids to all-over "field" paintings.

### Field In The Wind (2025, shown at Uprise Art), acrylic and Flashe on panel
URL: https://shop.upriseart.com/products/field-in-the-wind  (listed as larger than 40x60 in; exact size not given)
Local image: research/scott-sueme/field-in-the-wind.jpg, research/scott-sueme/field-in-the-wind-closeup.jpg (angled installation view)

- **Light source:** none is depicted. The light comes from the contrast of bright strokes on a near-black ground, so the greens look lit from within. Flashe is very matte, so nothing glares, and in the angled shot the surface reflects nothing.
- **Ground:** a flat, even navy-black of about `#020612` to `#03070f`. There is no gradient at all. The depth comes only from the density of the marks.
- **Marks:** hundreds of short, slightly curved strokes, all leaning about 5 to 12 degrees to the right, like grass bending in wind. They come in two greens: a bright `#0fa865`/`#01945e` and a darker, bluer `#016c54`/`#0a6e55`. The darker strokes fall back and the bright ones come forward, which gives two depth planes from two colours. The strokes are sign-painter clean: hard edges and rounded ends, with a little hand wobble in thickness.
- **Accents:** about 35 small white six-petal daisies (`#f1ede4`) with ochre centres (`#c69a3a`), scattered at random rather than on a grid. They are the only warm notes.
- **Edges and object:** the panel sits in a thin natural-oak float frame, with a narrow shadow gap between the panel and the frame. The oak's warm tan (about `#95704d` on the shaded side, lighter on the face) frames the dark field.
- **Web idea:** a dark hero (`#03070f`) filled with a procedural field of short rounded strokes (stroke width about 0.35% of viewport width, lengths 3 to 8%), each rotated 5 to 12 degrees clockwise and drawn in two greens. Put the darker green at about 60% of strokes so it reads as the back layer. Scatter a few tiny light glyphs as the only warm points. This is a texture, not an illustration: no gradients, no blur.

### Dream Screen (2025, shown at Uprise Art), acrylic, Flashe, spray paint and sawdust on panel
URL: https://shop.upriseart.com/products/dream-screen  (listed in the 25x31 to 40x60 in range)
Local image: research/scott-sueme/dream-screen.jpg

- **Ground:** matte blue-black, about `#101a1c` (slightly green). All the shapes float on it like cut paper.
- **The one real gradient:** a large rounded pill (stadium shape) in the upper right, sprayed as a smooth vertical gradient. It runs from emerald `#019d6e` at the top through `#018e90` to cerulean `#0177bb` at the bottom. The samples step almost evenly, so it is close to linear with a slight ease into the blue. It has no banding and a very fine spray grain. It is the only object in the painting that seems to glow. Because every other shape is flat, the gradient reads as light: a screen or a sky inside a window.
- **Other shapes:** a magenta `#ec01a3` hourglass with red-orange `#cb362c` cones; a lime `#c9fa71` trapezoid over slate `#37667c`, inside an olive `#a3a24b` box; an orange and teal (`#ef8a3a` / `#1f7fae`) interlocking block of triangles and arcs that vibrates, because the two are near-complements at similar value; a thin, hand-wobbly pale-yellow stick (`#f2de6a`).
- **Surface contrast:** a gold, spiky starburst shape is built up with sawdust into real relief. It casts tiny real shadows, so in the photo its texture reads as lit from the upper left. Flat Flashe fields sit next to one sculpted, textured shape.
- **Edges:** all hard, but cut by hand: straight edges drift slightly, and a few of the tapered points are not perfectly sharp.
- **Web idea:** among flat matte cards, make exactly one element a lit screen: a 180-degree gradient `#019d6e` → `#0177bb` with a 1 to 2% noise overlay (a spray-grain look), rounded at `border-radius: 50%/100%`. Every other element stays flat. This single gradient works as the page's light source.

### Emotional Mapping Geography (painted July 2021 to November 2023), acrylic, oil, vinyl emulsion, spray paint and pastel on reclaimed wood
URL: https://shop.upriseart.com/products/emotional-mapping-geography  (about 49 x 81 in, going by the gallery's image filename)
Local image: research/scott-sueme/emotional-mapping-geography.jpg

- **Composition:** a strict grid of 19 columns by 8 rows (152 small portrait-format tiles) on an off-white ground (`#e8e7e2`). Gutters are about 40% of the tile width. Each tile is its own tiny painting, a kind of daily record: stripes, dots, a leaf, a star, wood grain, impasto.
- **Gradients:** several tiles hold a single smooth vertical fade. One goes from pink `#d6507b` down to plum `#73334a`, another from green `#63a468` to deep green `#456b5f`, and others are teal-to-white or violet-to-dark. They sit next to hard-edge tiles, so the fades read as small light boxes in the grid.
- **Surface:** mixed on purpose. Some tiles are flat and matte, some carry impasto ridges, some have a pastel scrub. The ground itself is flat and quiet.
- **Web idea:** an index or archive page laid out as a strict grid of small cards (gutters about 40% of card width). Most cards are flat, but about 10% carry a single vertical two-stop gradient, and those become the lit cells. It works well for a changelog or a project index.

**Takeaways for the site:**
- Keep grounds perfectly flat and matte, and let one element hold the gradient. In Sueme's work the gradient is the light, so light is rationed.
- A two-tone depth trick: the same mark in a bright hue and a darker, cooler hue, with the darker one more numerous. That gives foreground and background with no blur or shadow.
- Hand-cut hard edges: slight irregularity in straight edges (for example, an SVG path with 0.3 to 0.6px jitter) keeps flat geometry from looking like a template.

## Anne Vieux

American painter. Her ground is a sublimation-dye print of scanned holographic or translucent paper on faux suede (later canvas). She paints acrylic ribbons and lines over it that copy or divert the printed patterns. All three 2017 works below come from her solo show "Mesh" at Annka Kultys Gallery, London (2017).

### wavelength (double vision series) (2017), acrylic on sublimation-dyed faux suede, traces, and frame
URL: https://www.annkakultys.com/exhibitions/mesh/  (89 x 132 cm)
Local image: research/anne-vieux/wavelength-2017.jpg

- **Light source:** the light seems to come from inside the print. The printed ground is a scan of crumpled holographic paper, so its brightest areas are near-white hotspots (`#dee7e9`, `#fae0db`) with no clear direction. ## Gabriele Evertz

Berlin-born New York painter of precise vertical stripe paintings. Grey is central to her work: black-and-white mixtures set against thin saturated lines. All works are acrylic on canvas and square.

### White Light (2017), acrylic on canvas, 72 x 72 in
URL: https://www.gabrieleevertz.com/2018_present.html
Local image: research/gabriele-evertz/white-light-2017.jpg

- **Light source:** the centre. About the middle 70% of the square is a field of near-white and pale warm-grey bands (`#e8e3df`, `#ebded3`, `#dfdce3`), crossed by thin saturated hairlines in orange, pink, violet, blue, green and cyan. The outer 12 to 15% on each side is dark: charcoal, black and plum bands (`#534c52`, `#5c4c5c`, `#565154`), with thin coloured lines inside the dark zone. Because the dark sits at the edges, the white centre seems to emit light. It works like a vignette built from stripes, not from a gradient.
- **Gradient behaviour:** stepped, not continuous. The move from dark edge to white centre takes about 5 to 6 stripes (roughly 4 to 5% of the width), passing through mid-greys (`#717689`, `#a5a8a2`) and warm tans (`#b6aca2`). It is roughly symmetrical, but the right side's dark zone has warmer brown-tan steps (`#948174`, `#a08f8c`).
- **Colour meeting:** each coloured line is 1 to 3 px wide at this resolution (maybe 3 to 5 mm on the real canvas) and meets the grey at a hard seam. On pale grey the thin lines look more intense than they are. Up close, the grey bands and white bands have slightly different widths, and some greys lean a few degrees off vertical, so the field shimmers.
- **Surface:** matte, even acrylic with no brushwork visible at this size. There is no reflection.
- **Web idea:** a full-bleed section built from 1px to 4px vertical stripes. The central 70% is a near-white stack (`#e8e3df` / `#efe9e3` / `#dfdce3` alternating), and the outer 15% on each side steps down to `#534c52` over about 6 stripes. Scatter saturated 1 to 2px lines (for example `#f07a2a`, `#e8457d`, `#3b5bd9`, `#35b27a`) through the whole width. The centre will read as a light source with no gradient anywhere.

### Grays + the Expanded Spectrum (2014), acrylic on canvas, 60 x 60 in
URL: https://www.gabrieleevertz.com/2014_2017.html
Local image: research/gabriele-evertz/grays-expanded-spectrum-2014.jpg

- **Composition:** a spectrum laid out left to right as thin saturated lines. It starts with cobalt and ultramarine at the left edge, then runs through cyan, teal, green, yellow-green, yellow, orange, red, crimson, magenta, pink and lavender, and ends back at ultramarine or violet on the right, so the spectrum loops around. The grey bands between the lines form a value arc: dark charcoal at both edges (`#4f5359`, `#4b4d54`), lightest in the green-to-yellow centre (`#e0e3e3`, `#f2f2f2`), with a second dark trough under the reds (`#403f4e`, `#584043`).
- **Colour meeting:** the greys next to each hue seem tinted by it (a grey by the green reads faintly green, a grey by the orange reads warm). Averaged samples show this (`#b4ccbc` near greens, `#ad9f99` near oranges), and it is a real optical effect in the image. The grey is neutral, and the neighbouring hue pulls it.
- **Gradient behaviour:** value runs in waves across the width (dark → light → dark → light → dark), in discrete stripe steps. It is not a smooth ramp.
- **Web idea:** a "spectrum ruler" divider or footer band. Place 20 to 30 hairlines in hue order across the width, with grey spacer bands whose lightness follows `L = 30% + 60% * sin(pi * x)`, so the band is lightest in the middle. ### Be Thou The Rainbow (2025), acrylic on canvas, 72 x 72 in
URL: https://www.gabrieleevertz.com/2020_2026.html
Local image: research/gabriele-evertz/be-thou-the-rainbow-2025.jpg

- **Structure:** evenly spaced coloured stripes on a grey ground. Looking closely, each stripe is two adjacent hues side by side, a pair of neighbours on the colour wheel: violet `#5a4477` + indigo `#44438a`; blue `#2f67a3` + deep teal `#04616d`; green `#379a3a` + yellow-green `#9fd163`; amber `#fbbe03` + orange `#fca200`; vermilion `#de3821` + crimson `#c50a29`. The two halves meet at a hard seam and vibrate against each other.
- **Ground gradient:** the grey is lighter in a central vertical zone (`#e0dedf`) and darker toward both sides (`#b3b3b4`). It is a soft, horizontally symmetric falloff, a smooth ease across the whole width with no banding, so the centre looks lit.
- **Tilt:** the stripes are not quite parallel. Many lean 0.5 to 1.5 degrees, and neighbours converge or diverge slightly, so the field seems to breathe or sway.
- **Edges:** crisp and taped, but some stripe edges show slight irregularity at full size.
- **Web idea:** vertical double stripes (two analogous hues at about 55/45 width, hard seam) spaced evenly across a grey ground with a horizontal radial falloff (`radial-gradient(ellipse 60% 140% at 50% 50%, #e2e0e0, #b3b3b4)`). **Takeaways for the site:**
- Evertz makes "light" through value placement, not gradients: dark at the edges and pale in the centre, with saturated hairlines that look brighter on pale grey.
- Double-hue stripes (analogous pairs with a hard seam) vibrate at the boundary. This is a very reproducible CSS pattern (`repeating-linear-gradient` with hard stops).
- A small, random off-vertical tilt keeps a rigorous stripe system from looking mechanical.

## Douglas Witmer

Philadelphia painter working in reductive geometric abstraction. His recent paintings are built from very thin, watercolour-like acrylic layers, many over black gesso. The compositions are mostly one horizontal division of the canvas.

### Ever an Ember (2022), acrylic on canvas, 60 x 48 in
URL: https://www.douglaswitmer.com/painting/

The top 59% is a dark oxblood-maroon (`#5e2f32` to `#683e3d`) and the bottom 41% is vermilion or blood-orange (`#e46547`, deepening to `#d04e36` toward the bottom edge). - **Edges and object:** the colour wraps over the canvas edges and the sides are painted. The canvas stands off the wall on a deep stretcher with a real shadow. There is no frame.
- **Web idea:** a horizon split at about 59% height, `#5e2f32` above and `#e46547` below. Blend them over about 3% of the height, and put a narrow darker overlap band just below the join (the orange mixed about 20% toward maroon: `#b35249`). ### Schule von Freiburg, floor installation (2016), epoxy resin poured on the floor, Museum für Neue Kunst, Freiburg
URL: https://peterzimmermann.com/boden/#installation-view-schule-von-freiburg-2016-epoxy-resin
Local image: research/peter-zimmermann/schule-von-freiburg-floor-2016.jpg

- **Reflection:** the whole floor is a mirror. The window mullions, door jambs and walls reflect as vertical streaks in the resin. The turquoise pool (`#028AA4` deep, `#77C7D2` shallow) looks like actual depth because the reflections pass straight over the colour.

The ring edges are crisp but low-contrast. Around it lies a very pale pink-grey resin (`#B6AAAC`), and the turquoise carries on through the doorway into the next room as a thin horizon strip.
- **Takeaway:** colour as a shallow liquid layer with the room reflected on top of it.

Takeaways for the site:
- **Hairline meniscus rim:** give layered translucent shapes a 1px rim 15–25% lighter than the fill (e.g. `rgba(255,235,245,.55)` on a `#160184` field), with a small brighter point where two rims cross. It is cheap to do with SVG strokes, or with a CSS `box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1px` on rounded divs.
- **Internal glow per shape, not global lighting:** fill each blob with an off-centre radial gradient, with the bright core at 25–35% from one edge (`radial-gradient(120% 90% at 30% 20%, #1D24AA, #020075 60%, #0A0020)`). Put a dark vignette on the page frame so the edges sink toward `#0A0020`.
- **Subtractive overlap:** stack translucent layers with `mix-blend-mode: multiply` (or `color-burn` for the darker Zimmermann overlaps) so a red over grey turns maroon rather than pink.
- **Floor-as-mirror:** a faint, blurred, low-opacity (8–12%) vertically flipped copy of the page's vertical elements laid over a coloured base reads as poured resin.

## Cross-artist notes (patterns shared by several artists)

- **Thin bright edges carry the light** in three of the five. Zimmermann has hairline meniscus rims, Rabchinskey has edge-lit acrylic, and Dávila's clear glass is visible only by its edge. A 1px lighter or more saturated edge on translucent layers is the most reusable move from this group.
- **Stacked translucency changes value in steps, not smoothly.** Dávila's smoked glass (1 layer → 2 layers), Rabchinskey's filters (red over orange → `#BE081D`) and Zimmermann's resin pours all behave like multiply blending.
- **Horizons:** Krauze's ruled frame over broken stones, Rabchinskey's treeline with filters straddling it, Dávila's banded marble and his rainbow quarter-arc. A hard horizontal line is the anchor for all the soft or broken material around it.
- **Matte versus gloss:** Martin and Krauze are fully matte, so their light is carried by colour or by a single gold accent. Zimmermann is gloss and reflective, so light lives in the surface. It is worth picking one mode per section rather than mixing them.
