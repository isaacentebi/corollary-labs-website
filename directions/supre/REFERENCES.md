# Tektonik (`supre`) — references

**Concept.** The home opens on a flat Suprematist picture: chalk, haze and slate planes held in tension on a night ground along one rising diagonal. A single cadmium bar (the agent) waits at the edge; as you scroll it crosses the grain and strikes the picture, which fractures along the cuts the planes always had. The same pieces, none removed, reassemble into a weightless axonometric construction around the agent (floors, a rod, masses, a mast; the move Malevich made from painting to *arkhitekton* and Hadid made from *arkhitekton* to building). The camera then pulls back and tilts the whole world, as in Hadid's *The World (89 Degrees)*: other flat compositions float across a fan-shaped field, and the reorganisation travels outward from one to the next, carrying a cadmium bar into each; the outer ones stay flat until you click one, and the change spreads from there, weakening with every step. What we borrowed is the compositional physics (diagonal tension, floating planes, scale jumps, the switch from picture plane to axonometric flight), not the red-and-black poster.

## Primary sources

- Kazimir Malevich, *Suprematist Composition: Airplane Flying* (1915), MoMA — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79269 — planes on one dominant diagonal, no ground and no gravity: the grammar of state A.
- Kazimir Malevich, *Suprematist Composition: White on White* (1918), MoMA — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80385 — a tilted square in infinite space; the restraint of the palette and the dark "infinite" ground.
- Kazimir Malevich, *Suprematism: 34 Drawings* (1920), MoMA — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/11073 — the same small vocabulary recombined page after page; the essay glyphs work this way.
- El Lissitzky, *Proun 19D* (1920/21), MoMA — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79040 — multiple viewpoints at once and axonometric volumes that float; state C and the camera tilt.
- Zaha Hadid, *Malevich's Tektonik* (1976–77), Zaha Hadid Foundation — https://www.zhfoundation.com/collections/malevichs-tektonik/ (SFMOMA: https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/98.151/) — a Malevich *arkhitekton* laid out as horizontal floors; the stacked, fanned slabs of the reassembled state.
- Zaha Hadid, *The Peak* (1982–83), MoMA — https://www.moma.org/collection/works/202 — shards and cantilevered beams splintered by a force and hovering; the fracture state.
- Zaha Hadid, *The World (89 Degrees)* (1983) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_works_by_Zaha_Hadid — the whole world tilted and fanned out from one point; the rolled camera and fan-shaped field of state D.

## Awwwards

- Igloo Inc (Abeto × Bureaux), Site of the Year — https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc — a three-section scroll that stays cinematic because geometry breaks apart and recombines as you scroll; taken: short story, state-to-state reassembly, restraint of palette.
- Lusion v3, Site of the Day and Site of the Year — https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lusion-v3 — real-time objects with weight and inertia that react to the pointer; taken: pointer tilt and local "lift" of planes under the cursor, damped scroll.
- Lusion WebGL scroll navigation (Awwwards inspiration) — https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/webgl-scroll-navigation-lusion — the camera itself as the narrative device; taken: a single continuous camera move (frontal → axonometric → pulled-back, tilted perspective) instead of scene cuts.

- Type: Archivo Variable (weight and width axes). Headlines at 125% width; links and titles widen from 100% to 125% under the pointer.
- Mark: a square, a bar across it, and a small cadmium bar arriving from outside; on hover the three re-order along the other diagonal.
