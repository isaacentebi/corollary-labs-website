## 1. The Public Domain Review: Golgi and Cajal plates
**URL:** https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/illustrations-of-the-nervous-system-golgi-and-cajal/
**What it is:** An image collection from an online journal of out-of-copyright works. It reproduces 19th-century stained nervous-tissue drawings: black silver-nitrate cells and red-ink networks on yellowed paper.
**Take:**
- The stain palette, as it stands. Near-black "hematoxylin" line, one red or rose accent for the thing that changed, and warm cream paper as the ground, not white.
- The plate-first layout. The centred title sits in Alegreya (a humanist serif), then a single large plate, then a narrow serif reading column (~660px, generous leading).
- Treat each diagram as a numbered plate with small italic letter labels (a, b, d), the way Cajal marks cells.
**Why it fits:** It is the exact histology-section look in the brief. It is also about networks of discrete cells, which is the argument about firms. And it reads as a natural-history record, not a product.
**Avoid:** The chrome: navy "Support" pill, row of social icons, shop bag, cookie box. Keep only the paper, plate and serif.
**Captures:** `pdr-golgi-cajal.png`, `pdr-golgi-cajal-plate.png`

## 2. inconvergent (Anders Hoff): "On Generative Algorithms: Hyphae"
**URL:** https://inconvergent.net/generative/hyphae/ (see also https://inconvergent.net/generative/differential-line/)
**What it is:** A generative artist's essay on growing branching structures, with the algorithm explained in prose next to the outputs.
**Take:**
- The growth rule itself. Append circles along a wobbling heading. Each new node shrinks a little. Branches spawn roughly perpendicular to the parent and die when they collide.
- Line weight falls with generation, so trunks are heavy and tips are hair-fine. That gives the "drawn by a hand" taper for mycelial links between firms.
- Differential-line gives the membrane: a closed curve that inserts nodes where it grows fastest and folds into a cell outline.
**Why it fits:** Growth by local rules, with no designed layout: the "growth, not machinery" thesis in code. It also shows how to draw the network in pure black line on white, which ports easily to violet ink on paper.
**Avoid:** The site shell is bare Fira sans with all-caps nav. Take the drawing, not the typography.
**Capture:** `inconvergent-hyphae.png`

## 3. Sage Jenson: "36 Points" (Physarum explorer)
**URL:** https://sagejenson.com/36points/ (write-up of the model: https://cargocollective.com/sagejenson/physarum)
**What it is:** A full-screen, real-time Physarum (slime-mould) simulation by the artist whose physarum work NASA cited. Keys a–z and 0–9 jump between 36 parameter presets.
**Take:**
- The two-layer model from the write-up. Agents sense a trail map, deposit onto it, and the map diffuses and decays. That produces self-reinforcing transport tubes: the right primitive for "change spreading through the economy".
- The grainy, stippled density rendering, where tubes are made of millions of points and not stroked lines. Printed in violet on cream, it would read as a stained section.
- The interaction model: the page *is* the organism. The only UI is one small monospace caption box, and keys morph the whole field between states. A section of the site could switch presets on scroll the same way.
**Why it fits:** It is grown, not drawn, and the network routes itself. That matches the idea of agents finding paths through a firm.
**Avoid:** Grey-on-black at full-bleed intensity reads as noise or cosmic; it would swamp text. Keep it sparse, pale and behind type.
**Captures:** `sagejenson-36points.png`, `sagejenson-physarum.png`

## 4. Distill: "Growing Neural Cellular Automata"
**URL:** https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/
**What it is:** An interactive research article by Mordvintsev, Randazzo, Niklasson (Google) and Michael Levin (Tufts). It trains a neural network to be the local rule of a cellular automaton that grows, keeps and repairs a shape.
**Take:**
- The interaction: click to wound the organism and watch it regenerate, then double-click to plant a new seed cell. That is a direct model for "insert an agent into a firm and watch the tissue reorganise".
- The controls in plain language: Growing / Persistent / Regenerating as three modes of one model, with a speed slider and step counter.
- The explorable-essay structure: a live figure at the top, then numbered experiments, each with its own small live figure.
**Why it fits:** It is the closest subject match on the web: AI as a *local rule inside cells*, producing organism-level change. It carries scientific credibility without looking corporate.
**Avoid:** Distill's default look (system sans, navy bar, emoji target picker, pixel-grid rendering). Take the mechanic, not the skin.
**Captures:** `distill-growing-ca.png`, `distill-growing-ca-body.png`

## 5. Emergence Magazine
**URL:** https://emergencemagazine.org/ (example reading page: https://emergencemagazine.org/essay/a-universal-lesson-of-the-seven-sisters-story/)
**Award (verified):** Awwwards Site of the Day, 11 Aug 2018, by Studio Airport (https://www.awwwards.com/sites/emergence-magazine).
**What it is:** A designer-led publication on ecology, culture and spirituality.
**Take:**
- The type pairing. Bradford (a soft book serif) at large reading size for body text, and GT America in wide-tracked small caps for labels and nav. Humane, not technical.
- The reading-progress hairline under the fixed header, plus the essay title in the header once you scroll. A calm way to navigate long argument pages.
- Full-bleed artwork as the opener of each story, then a cut straight to a single centred text column.
**Why it fits:** It proves that long-form thinking about living systems can be the whole site: no features, no CTAs. The tone Corollary needs for essays on firms and the economy.
**Avoid:** The current homepage hero is a soft, grainy rainbow gradient, which is exactly the "gradient blob" the founder bans. Also avoid white-on-black body text; stay on warm paper.
**Captures:** `emergence.png` (gradient hero, as an anti-example), `emergence-stories.png`, `emergence-essay.png`, `aw-emergence.png`

## 6. OXMAN
**URL:** https://www.oxman.com/
**What it is:** Neri Oxman's design company, which combines engineered design with biological growth. The homepage runs a single living-lab video under minimal typography.
**Take:**
- The aperture. One hairline circle drawn over the full-bleed footage, like a microscope field or a cell membrane, framing the organism inside the page. A very cheap way to say "you are looking through a lens".
- Ultra-thin display lettering that draws itself letter by letter on scroll ("DESIGN THAT UNITES NAT…" is caught mid-reveal). Growth expressed in type.
- Only three entry points (Coproduction / Cohabitation / Communication), each phrased as a question, not a feature.
**Why it fits:** The founder of "material ecology" frames a company as a living system with almost no product language. The membrane-as-frame device carries straight over.
**Avoid:** Hairline grotesk over dark video is cold and luxury-tech. Swap it for a humanist serif on paper. Also avoid lab-coat stock footage.
**Captures:** `oxman.png`, `oxman-scroll.png`
