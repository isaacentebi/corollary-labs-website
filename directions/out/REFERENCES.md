## Awwwards

1. **Igloo Inc**, by abeto & Bureaux. https://www.igloo.inc/
   Awwwards Site of the Day (23 Jul 2024), Developer Award, Site of the Year 2024. https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc
   *What we take:* the whole site is one continuous object that you move through, not a stack of pages. It uses a two-tone palette (`#b6bac5` / `#383e4e`) and puts all of its craft into a single material. For us that material is the sheet: every page is a region of it.

2. **Accordion**, by HOLOGRAPHIK & Ilja van Eck. https://www.accordion.net.au/
   Awwwards Site of the Day (29 Aug 2024). https://www.awwwards.com/sites/accordion
   *What we take:* panels and masks that "contract and expand" instead of cutting between pages. We push that into the navigation model itself: the open region stretches and the others fold to strips that never leave the screen, so the page is never replaced.

3. **Lusion v3**, by Lusion. https://lusion.co/
   Awwwards Site of the Day (2 Oct 2023). Its Dev Award gave animations and transitions a perfect 10. https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lusion-v3
   *What we take:* a single WebGL layer that responds to the cursor under calm DOM typography, and transitions that feel physical. Our shader is much smaller (one full-screen pass, two gratings), and the DOM stays readable and accessible.

4. **Typography Principles**, by Obys. https://typographyprinciples.obys.agency/
   Awwwards Site of the Day (28 Dec 2020). https://www.awwwards.com/sites/typography-principles
   *What we take:* very large type used as the image. Our version runs the type through the variable width axis of Anybody (50–150 %). Labels in the folds are condensed and open titles are expanded; hovering stretches a word, and three links behave like a small version of the sheet.

## Beyond Awwwards: optics, op art, net art

5. **Moiré patterns: the optics.** Michael Bach, *Optical Illusions & Visual Phenomena*. https://michaelbach.de/ot/lum-moire1/ See also Gerald Oster's moiré work (the biophysicist behind much 1960s "optical art") and Carsten Nicolai, *Moiré Index* (Gestalten, 2010).
   *What we take:* the fact that makes the direction work. When two identical gratings are superimposed and one is deformed, the fringes are the contour lines of the deformation. An undisturbed sheet looks plain. A sheet that has been reorganised shows exactly where and how much, with no labels. Moiré strain analysis is a real engineering method for measuring the deformation of a surface.

Not taken: hero with a CTA, feature grids, stock 3D, gradient blobs. None of the references is a product landing page.
