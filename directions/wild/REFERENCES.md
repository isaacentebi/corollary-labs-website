# Wild — "Tableau": references

Captures are local only, in `research/wild/` (gitignored). Nothing here is published.

1. **Ryoji Ikeda, *datamatics* / *test pattern*** — https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/datamatics/ and https://www.ryojiikeda.com/project/testpattern/
   Take: data as the whole material of the picture; black ground, white cells and numerals, one colour used only when something changes; density as a feeling, not as decoration. Why: the company's subject is structural and quantitative, and Ikeda shows that a grid of values can be monumental on its own, with no illustration on top. We take the discipline (black, white, one signal colour, tabular numerals), not the strobe.

2. **Jacques Bertin, the reorderable matrix ("Dominos", 1968)** — https://dataphys.org/list/bertins-reorderable-matrices/
   Take: reorganisation drawn as permutation. Rows and columns of a table slide into a new order; the entries are unchanged, yet a new block structure appears. Why: this is the most literal picture of "reorganisation, not replacement" we found. Nothing is swapped out; the same parts are rearranged and the structure is new. It also gives the motion a mechanical honesty: rows travel as whole rows.

3. **Bartosz Ciechanowski, explorable articles (e.g. *Moon*)** — https://ciechanow.ski/moon/
   Take: the reader causes the phenomenon instead of watching it; precision in every label; one model reused across every figure. Why: our hero is a working table. Hover reads any cell; a click sends a change through it round by round. The whole story is one object in successive states, like his single simulated world.

4. **igloo.inc** (Awwwards Site of the Year 2024) — https://igloo.inc/
   Take: one coherent WebGL object that the camera travels through, with a cold, exact palette and a glyph-based (ASCII) loader; a sense of instrument, not brochure. Why: permission to make the home page one continuous 3D object (the table lifts into a height field at the end) while staying fast, and to let type be small and technical around a large object.

5. **Nicholas Felton, Feltron Annual Reports** — http://feltron.com/
   Take: a self-portrait made entirely of tables of the subject's own quantities. Why: it is how our company introduces itself. On About, Corollary Labs is described in its own ontology: one column of a table, its inputs and its outputs (with placeholders where the facts are not settled), instead of a mission statement.

Conceptual (not a site, and not quoted on the page): the input–output table of an economy (each column a sector's inputs, each row a sector's sales; the diagonal is what a sector supplies to itself), and the power series of its inverse, I + A + A² + …, in which each power is one more round of an effect passing through suppliers. This is the grammar the direction borrows. No historical names appear on the site.
