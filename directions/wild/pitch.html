Tableau

The site is one input–output table of an economy, drawn as a live grid of cells on a black ground. Each column is a firm and each cell is an input it takes from another firm; three extra rows at the foot are what people supply: execution, plans, objectives. The story is told as successive states of that one table. An agent enters a firm and its execution and plans move out of the supplied rows into the firm's own diagonal cell. Then the whole table permutes into a new order (the same entries, a new structure: reorganisation, not replacement), the change passes through the table in rounds from trading partner to trading partner, and at the end the table lifts into a height field and deforms continuously from the old economy to the new one: a homotopy you can watch.

01Why this and not the others

Plotter draws the firm as a machine on paper; Organic grows it; Proof proves it. Tableau measures it. It takes the company's ontology literally at the level of economics rather than illustration: "a firm is defined by its inputs and outputs" is exactly what a column of an input–output table is, and "automation is a firm producing its own input" is exactly mass moving onto the diagonal. Reorganisation is a permutation of rows and columns (Bertin's reorderable matrix). Diffusion is the power series I + A + A² + …, each power one more round through suppliers. The homotopy is H(t) = (1 − t)A₀ + tA₁, drawn, not named. None of this is decoration; every frame is a real operation on a real kind of object.

03Page structure

  1. Home: one sticky WebGL stage (instanced cells, one scroll progress). States 0–6 above. Hover any cell to read row, column and value; click a column to send a change through the table in rounds. Ends at the index of pages.
  2. Essays: the index is a table (number, title, date, sections, notes, minutes), sortable by column headers. Essay pages keep the long-reading template, set in Newsreader on the dark ground, with a thin table of contents in mono.
  3. About: the company described in its own ontology, as one column of a table: its inputs and its outputs (Essays: 3, true today; the rest in [brackets]), then a colophon.
  4. Team: a table of people, rows with [Name] [Role] [one line].
  5. Contact: one row: email, location, and [placeholder] for how to write.

Navigation: a thin header row that reads like the column headings of a table (00 Index · 01 Essays · 02 About · 03 Team · 04 Contact). This breaks Plotter's "no navbar" rule on purpose: in a table the headings are part of the object, and the pages are its columns. The bottom-right corner is kept free for the version switcher.

04How the company introduces itself

Not with a statement about itself. The name is set large under the table, like the title of a statistical table, and the first thing the visitor can do is read a cell. The About page then describes Corollary Labs the way the site describes every firm: a column of inputs and outputs, with placeholders where the facts are not settled. The only sentence in its own voice is the existing "Corollary Labs is a company." followed by bracketed placeholders.

08Motion and craft

  • WebGL2 instanced cells via three.js (one draw call, ~1,100 cells). Rows and columns travel as whole rows and columns, never as scattered particles. Easing is mechanical (expo in-out), like a rod mechanism.
  • Reduced motion: every state is rendered as a still, scroll switches between them without tweening, no flicker.
  • 375px: the table stays square and fills the width; captions sit under it; hover becomes tap.
  • New copy (captions) comes from a clean-room writer and is flagged for founder review.
