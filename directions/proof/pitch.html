Proof

The site is typeset as a short mathematics paper whose figures are live. A firm is drawn the way category theorists draw a process: a box with typed wires going in and coming out (a string diagram). Its grammar gives the rest of the story exact form. Automation is the boundary being redrawn around the process that makes one of the firm's inputs. Replacing a box with another box of the same type changes nothing about the firm's inputs and outputs; rewiring does. And the founder's isotopy becomes a theorem, not a metaphor: by Joyal and Street, any continuous deformation of a string diagram in the plane denotes the same morphism, so the page can show, literally, the difference between moving things around and reorganising.

Why this and not Plotter

Plotter tells the story as one sticky stage with captions. Proof is read: numbered statements in a paper, each owning its own figure, which the reader scrubs by scrolling or dragging. Every sentence on the home page is either a definition, a theorem with a citation, a corollary that follows from it, or a remark that is true by construction. The strangeness comes from the formalism, not from adjectives. It breaks three Plotter defaults, deliberately: (1) numbered statements (the founder rejected numbered sections on About; here the numbering is the content, because statements refer to each other); (2) a margin navigation (a dependency graph of the statements, not a navbar); (3) blue instead of green, cream instead of white, one inverted plate.

Page structure

  1. Title plate. Full screen. A large string diagram fills the page: some forty wires crossing the screen, passing through small solid boxes. The pointer deforms it by an isotopy of the plane (wires are pushed, never cut). The name, set large in Libertinus Display. One margin note: Fig. 0. Moving the pointer deforms this diagram by a planar isotopy; by Theorem 3.1 the morphism it denotes is unchanged.
  2. Front matter. How the company introduces itself: an Abstract (clean-room copy, flagged), Keywords, and two real classification lines: JEL O33, L23 and MSC2020 18M30, 18M35. Dense, true and specific without stating the business model.
  3. §1 Firms. Definition 1.1: a firm is a morphism f : X → Y. Fig. 1 is a single box; hovering a wire shows its type.
  4. §2 Automation. Definition 2.1: given f : A ⊗ P → Y and g : O → P, the automation of P is the passage to f ∘ (1 ⊗ g) : A ⊗ O → Y. Fig. 2: scroll moves the blue boundary over g; the type under the figure is recomputed from the wires that cross the boundary. Remark 2.2: plans and objectives.
  5. §3 Replacement and reorganisation (centrepiece). Theorem 3.1 (Joyal–Street). Corollary 3.2: moving boxes and wires without cutting them does not change the morphism. Remark 3.3: replacing a box by one of the same type keeps the firm's type. Remark 3.4: cutting and rejoining wires is not an isotopy. Fig. 3 is pinned while the reader scrolls through four states: the firm; a box replaced (type unchanged); the diagram deformed (type unchanged); surgery (coordination removed, a feedback loop, new outputs; type changed). The only readout on the page is the type signature, because it is the invariant.
  6. §4 Propagation (the inverted plate). Remark 4.1: a composite g ∘ f is defined only when the outputs of f match the inputs of g. Fig. 4: an economy as one large diagram of firms; one firm changes type and the wires around it are rejoined, then the ones around those, at uneven times.
  7. End. A large ∎. Then References: the citations, followed by the site itself as references: Essays, About, Team, Correspondence.

Other pages. Essays as a journal's table of contents (number, title, abstract, date, reading time); essay pages typeset like papers with sidenotes. About as front matter: statement, classification, the construction of the mark, colophon. Team as an author block with numbered affiliations. Contact as "Correspondence". Navigation: a small mark top-left (home), the dependency-graph rail on the home page, and the References section at the foot of every page. Bottom-right stays empty for the version switcher.

Ink on cream, and one ultramarine used only for what changes: the redrawn boundary, a rewired wire, the current statement. Solid boxes are ink (the black box); outlined boxes are processes still supplied from outside. Byrne's discipline: solid vs dashed, two weights. Blue keeps the version apart from Plotter (green) and from Organic (warm natural palette).

One family, the family of printed papers (Libertinus descends from Linux Libertine, the face of ACM proceedings). It avoids the Computer Modern cliché while reading unmistakably as a journal.

Logo options

A. Trace (proposed)

The string diagram of a trace, Tr(f): a black box whose output is returned as its own input. Solid box = the black box and the Halmos end-of-proof ∎.

B. Turnstile

⊢, the sign for derivability: Γ ⊢ φ reads "φ follows from Γ". The literal meaning of the name as a glyph. Very quiet; strongest as a favicon.

C. Wordmark and ∎

The name followed by the end-of-proof mark. A corollary is what comes right after the ∎. Pure typography; the mark can stand alone.
