# Score — references

**Concept.** The home page is a graphic score read in time. On load the playhead plays one moment: a new voice enters, from nothing (a niente circle and an opening hairpin, which is also the logo), in the narrowest gap of a six-voice ensemble. Each voice is written in the grammar of a different graphic score (Xenakis string fan, Cardew line, Brown bars, Feldman boxes, Stockhausen bands, points), played material in ink and material to come in pencil. Scrolling moves the playhead on. The ensemble makes room, the brackets regroup around the new voice, the events settle onto a shared pulse, and each voice restates the new voice's figure in its own grammar: the fan pinches into a bundle and traces it, the Cardew line thickens, the points run, the Feldman boxes step register, the Brown bars stair-step, the bands follow. The only colour is the performer's highlighter: thick on the new part, thin under each restatement. Then the view pulls back (D) to other systems, which the figure reaches in order of distance. The view then returns to the finished score, and its time ruler settles at the foot of the screen, where it becomes the rule that opens the About section. Visitors can draw their own voice (after UPIC and Oramics). Space bends smoothly around the drawn line, and no voice ever crosses another. On touch screens, Draw is a mode that holds the page still. Sound is off by default. When on, it plays only short, quantised events in each voice's own timbre as the playhead crosses them, and the pentatonic set changes by one note after the entry.

## Primary sources (the lineage)

- Cornelius Cardew, *Treatise* (1963–67). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatise_(music). Borrowed: one continuous line that runs through the whole work, with thickness as the only dynamic. Voice 2 is this line, and the system bracket and time ruler come from its framing.
- Iannis Xenakis, *Metastaseis* (1953–54) and its graphic sketch. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metastaseis_(Xenakis). Borrowed: string glissandi drawn as ruled straight segments that fan out and converge, and continuous change drawn as glides rather than steps. Voice 1 is this fan, and all re-harmonisation is continuous deformation.
- Xenakis, UPIC (CEMAMu, 1977). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPIC and https://www.centre-iannis-xenakis.org/cix_upic_presentation?lang=en. Borrowed: the drawn line as the instrument. The hero's draw interaction turns a stroke into a voice, and when sound is on the stroke plays as a glide.
- Earle Brown, *December 1952* (from *Folio*). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earle_Brown. Borrowed: horizontal and vertical bars of varying thickness on an open field. This is voice 5.
- Morton Feldman, the *Projection* graph scores (1950–51). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morton_Feldman. Borrowed: boxes on a time grid for high, middle and low register, each holding a numeral for how many sounds. This is voice 4.
- Karlheinz Stockhausen, *Studie II* (1954). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studie_II. Borrowed: overlapping translucent rectangles as frequency bands along a measured time axis. This is voice 6, and the tick ruler comes from the same score.
- Daphne Oram, Oramics (1957–). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oramics and https://www.daphneoram.org/oramicsmachine/. Borrowed: parallel strips read in sync by one scanning line, and drawn shapes as control data. The playhead reads all voices at once.
- General notation logic: rehearsal marks (boxed letters A–G continue from the hero into the page sections and essay headings), dal niente with a hairpin (the entry mark and the logo), divisi (a voice splitting in two), and the performer's highlighter (the single accent colour).

## Awwwards references (craft and motion)

- Patatap, Site of the Day, 15 Apr 2014. https://www.awwwards.com/sites/patatap. Every input is one audiovisual event, and shape and sound are one gesture. Taken for the event marks and for the rule that sound only answers what you do (it never plays by itself).
- Audiograph, Site of the Day and Developer Award, 20 May 2016. https://www.awwwards.com/sites/audiograph. Each track gets its own generated drawing. Taken for the seeded miniature scores: each essay, the About page and each Team row gets its own system, rendered at build time.
- Orchestre de Paris, Resonance, Site of the Day, 17 Nov 2015. https://www.awwwards.com/sites/orchestre-de-paris-resonance. An orchestra shown as voices that react to one another. Taken for the ensemble reading and for keeping the interface quiet so the score carries the page.

## Palette, type, mark

- Paper `#f3f3ef` (cool staff-paper white), ink `#111213` (engraving black), pencil `#a6a8aa` (the unplayed future), rule `#d4d5d0`, and one highlighter yellow `#ffe14a` laid with multiply. The yellow marks the new part, its restatements, hovers and reading progress.
- Type: Bodoni Moda (display, optical sizes) for titles, the statement and leads. It is the engraved title page of a music edition, set against radical notation. Newsreader for essay text, Inter Tight for labels (small, tracked, uppercase, like instrument names). Font files are vendored in `site/src/fonts` (OFL).
- Mark: a niente circle and an opening hairpin, highlighted inside: a voice entering from silence.
- Wayfinding: each page has a rehearsal letter (About E, Essays F, Team G, Contact H, continuing the hero's A–D). Essay sections are lettered, and the reading ruler at the top of an essay is a playhead.
