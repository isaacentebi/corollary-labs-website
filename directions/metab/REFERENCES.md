# Metabolism (sketch) — references

**Concept.** The home page is one live axonometric drawn in the grammar of 1960s Metabolist drawings: service cores (shafts) carry infrastructure (beams), and self-contained units plug into sockets along them. Nothing is ever removed. As you scroll, lamp-coloured units are craned in and plugged, a new core rises, new infrastructure grows, and some existing units unplug and re-seat on the new beams, so the organisation reorganises around what arrived. Then the camera pulls back and the axonometric flattens into a city plan: a linear spine with branches (the Tokyo Bay plan). The same plug-in-and-grow change travels along that network, cluster by cluster. The visitor can plug units in (click), turn the drawing (drag), and each plug sends a pulse back along its beam and up the core. Borrowed: the plug-in/capsule logic, growth over time, the joint-core "tree" (Isozaki), the linear spine with branches (Tange), and the plan-oblique hidden-line drawing. Not borrowed: concrete, photos, retro colour.

## Primary sources (lineage)

- Kisho Kurokawa, Nakagin Capsule Tower (1970–72), capsule A1305 at MoMA: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/434291 . Units are prefabricated and bolted onto two cores so they can be exchanged. This is where the unit and its round window come from.
- MoMA, "The Many Lives of the Nakagin Capsule Tower": https://www.moma.org/calendar/exhibitions/5830 . Kurokawa's working drawings, including an axonometric of one capsule: the line weight and the way a unit is drawn.
- Arata Isozaki, Joint Core System, Shinjuku (elevation, 1960), MoMA: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/815 . Round cores with beams stacked at alternating directions, growing in any direction. This is how the cantilevers alternate by level.
- Arata Isozaki, Clusters in the Air sketches, CCA: https://www.cca.qc.ca/en/search/details/collection/object/88639 . Trunks, branches and units ("leaves") added over time; the trees join into a forest. This is the city phase.
- Kenzo Tange, A Plan for Tokyo 1960: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism_(architecture) (Tokyo Bay section) . A linear civic spine across the bay with perpendicular branches. The spine and branches in the city plan come from here.
- Kenzo Tange, Yamanashi Press and Broadcasting Centre (1966): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenz%C5%8D_Tange (Yamanashi Press and Broadcasting Centre) . Service cylinders with floors spanning between them and gaps left open for later floors. This is the hero structure: four cores, one still to rise.
- Kiyonori Kikutake, Marine City (1958): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyonori_Kikutake . Towers as masts that units attach to. It also informs the reading of the core as the part that stays while the units change.

## Awwwards

- Igloo Inc, Site of the Year: https://www.awwwards.com/sites/igloo-inc . A crystalline, modular object rendered in real time that flows straight into the rest of the page. What I took: one precise constructed object as the whole hero, and a scroll that transforms it instead of cutting away.
- Messenger by Abeto, Site of the Day and Site of the Year 2025: https://www.awwwards.com/sites/messenger . A tiny complete world you can turn, which is calm and has one clear thing to do. What I took: a small world the visitor can rotate and change with one gesture, with no UI chrome.

## Identity

- Palette: night graphite `#121418` ground, chalk line `#E8E4DA`, one lamp yellow for units that plug in (`#E0AE3C`, with face tones `#F2CD6A` / `#C7952B`). The colour is a lit window at night. Lamp appears only on what has plugged in (and on link rules).
- Type: Archivo variable (width axis). The name and headings are set expanded (125%) in caps, and body text at normal width. IBM Plex Mono is used for dates and numbers.
- Mark: an axonometric core with one lamp unit plugged into its side (`site/src/components/Mark.astro`, favicon).
