Storyboard

Five static frames for the home-page diagram, drawn before any animation so we can agree on the picture first. The only green thing is automation: the agent, and whatever it touches. Grey dashed means dying. Each frame lists which earlier design it borrows from and which source idea it draws.

FIG. 1 — THE FIRM

Work comes in, passes from person to person, and leaves as one output. Every step is told what to do by one centre: the bundles are instructions. The layout is built around that centre, like machines belted to a steam engine's line shaft.

Borrowed from: the hatched firm and the throughput dots (v1) · inputs and outputs (the new-combination design). Source: David, the line-shaft factory · Superdark, a firm is defined by its inputs and outputs.

FIG. 2 — AUTOMATION ENTERS

Automation arrives from outside and enters the firm. It doesn't sit outside as a supplier. It becomes an agent at one step. The person there stands aside. Nothing else changes: same instructions, same layout, same output. The new motor bolted into the old factory. This is where most firms are now.

Borrowed from: automation entering as a green line and staying inside (the new-combination design) · the green agent circle (v1). Source: David, the first electric motor dropped into the engine's place, with productivity flat for decades.

FIG. 3 — WHAT THE AGENT NEEDS

The agent's input changes. That step used to need a bundle of instructions saying how. The agent needs only a goal saying what. The how has moved inside. That's automation in Superdark's sense: the loom stopped needing hands, and the agent stops needing a plan.

Borrowed from: execution moving inside the boundary (the rejected objective/plan design, now shown on one line instead of three chips). Source: Superdark, automation as a factory's production of its own input, and the Class 1 → Class 2 step (plans → objectives).

FIG. 4 — REORGANISATION

The firm is rebuilt around agents instead of around the old centre. The coordination centre and its instructions die out, and every step becomes an agent. People move to the edges: they set goals going in and review what comes out, and a feedback loop connects the two. The boundary is redrawn as a different shape (the old outline is dashed) and left open on the right. The old output fades, and new outputs B and C appear that the old layout couldn't make.

Borrowed from: the green feedback loop (v1) · things dying in grey, the old route fading, new outputs appearing (the new-combination design) · the boundary redrawn and reopened. Source: David, the unit-drive factory rebuilt around the flow of work, where the gains came · Superdark, self-optimisation as a feedback loop · Schumpeter, new combinations replacing old ones.

FIG. 5 — DIFFUSION

Pull back to the field. Firm after firm reorganises the same way, slowly and unevenly. Behind the front, firms carry the loop. Ahead of it, the old layouts hold, and some die. Then the coda: the whole surface re-forms.

Borrowed from: the diffusion field and the homotopy coda (v1, unchanged). Source: diffusion as an S-curve with a persistent gap between leaders and the rest (OECD, "The Best versus the Rest").
